# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Singapore-issued American Express Platinum cardholders planning dining and hotel benefits, primarily on mobile phones. They use the explorer to understand current participating venues, compare options, check cached availability, and verify what changed before making a booking decision, often while travelling or making near-term plans.

## Product Purpose

Provide a practical, source-backed view of Platinum dining and hotel benefits that makes freshness, uncertainty, and changes visible. Success means a user can quickly find a relevant benefit, understand when its data was checked, see meaningful before-and-after updates, and follow the official source before acting.

## Brand Personality

Calm, premium, auditable. The product should feel considered and trustworthy without imitating American Express marketing or creating artificial urgency.

## Anti-references

- Notification centres dominated by red badges, alarm language, or engagement pressure.
- Promotional card-benefit pages that hide source dates, exclusions, or uncertainty.
- Dense admin dashboards that expose scraper internals instead of user-relevant changes.
- Decorative luxury styling that weakens readability or makes standard controls unfamiliar.

## Design Principles

- Show evidence, not vague status: every public update includes before, after, detection time, and source.
- Separate observation from interpretation: a missing listing is reported without inventing a reason.
- Keep attention proportional: surface meaningful reviewed changes without turning routine refreshes into alerts.
- Preserve the decision trail: later refreshes must not erase the change record a user relied on.
- Make the next action obvious: connect each update to the relevant explorer section and official source.
- Design mobile-first: prioritize one-handed use, compact summaries, large touch targets, and progressive disclosure before adapting the experience to wider screens.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Support keyboard navigation, screen readers, visible focus, colour-independent status cues, responsive layouts, and reduced-motion preferences. Use plain language and explicit dates suitable for an international audience.
